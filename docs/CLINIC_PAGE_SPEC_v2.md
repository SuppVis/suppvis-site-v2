## Page goal and audience

One page that makes a clinician understand, in under three minutes, that SuppVis hands them the patient's full between-visit story before the visit starts. Primary reader: individual practitioners (NDs, MDs, DOs, NPs), with naturopathic and integrative practices as the first audience. Primary CTA: book a demo. Secondary: join the founding-practice waitlist. The page wins by sounding like it was written by people who respect clinical judgment, because it was.

## Section 2: The blind spot (problem framing)

Header: **A visit is a snapshot. A condition is a film.**

Copy: Patients forget. They under-report. They don't connect the supplement they started in week two with the symptom that appeared in week three. By the time they're in your office, a month of signal has collapsed into "I think it was worse a couple weeks ago." You take history from recall. SuppVis replaces recall with record.

Three stat-style cards:
- Every day captured: supplements, medications, habits, food, incidents, biometrics, and daily scores
- Screened continuously: every logged combination checked against our clinically reviewed interaction library
- Grounded in evidence: 33,000+ peer-reviewed studies behind the platform, browsable publicly

## Section 3: The pre-visit brief (the centerpiece, replaces v1's generic portal section)

Header: **The brief reads the way you think.**

Intro line: Before each visit, the portal assembles one document from the patient's record, ordered the way a clinician works a case: what's going on, what changed, what they take, the whole person, then what's still unknown.

Walkthrough, presented as a scrolling annotated mockup of the brief with six numbered callouts:

1. **At a glance.** Flagged incidents, the biggest score changes since the last visit, check-in coverage, and open flags, so the likely reasons for the visit surface first.
2. **Incidents, and what changed before them.** When a patient logs something out of the ordinary, SuppVis looks backward: items started, stopped, or missed; sleep and mood shifts; the patient's own "anything different" notes. It proposes the timing associations around each incident, labeled as associations. Which ones matter is your call.
3. **Regimen: plan versus actual.** Your treatment plan items stand apart from anything the patient added on their own. Every medication, supplement, and peptide with dose, frequency, and days actually taken. Interactions flagged as logged.
4. **The whole person.** One row per domain of the patient's life, marked stable or changed, acute or chronic: sleep, nutrition and hydration, movement, mind and mood, substances, environment and cycles, gut, medications and supplements. The determinants a thorough intake covers, pre-assembled from the record.
5. **What's supporting them, and what's in the way.** The habits and items associated with their better days, and the items most often skipped, the gaps in logging, the barriers they've noted.
6. **Questions for the visit.** Generated from what the record doesn't show, so intake time goes to the gaps, not to retelling history.

Closing line for the section: The brief stops where clinical judgment starts. Diagnosis and the treatment plan stay with you.

## Section 4: The patient's side

Header: **Simple enough that patients actually do it.**

Copy: Patients use the SuppVis app: a daily check-in that takes under a minute. They log what they take and what they do, score how they feel against their goals, note anything out of the ordinary with a severity and a note, and answer one question that catches what every intake misses: "Did you do anything different today?" Travel, a new food, a brutal week at work, a missed night of sleep. The variation that explains change.

Sub-points (three short tiles):
- **Your plan, tagged.** After a visit, the treatment plan lives in the app with every item marked as yours. The patient follows it or doesn't, and you see which. Anything they add on their own is tracked separately as an extra.
- **Their data flows in.** Wearable biometrics (sleep, HRV, activity) and Apple Health data fill the record automatically, so the patient logs less and the brief sees more.
- **They stay in control.** The patient owns their SuppVis account and chooses to share it with you by invite code. They can stop sharing at any time. [Keep this framing exactly; it is the privacy model counsel is reviewing, and it must be described as patient-directed sharing, not as a practice data system. No HIPAA claims.]

## Section 5: Vignette one, the pregnancy journey

Header: **Nine months. Twelve visits. Two hundred seventy days of signal.**

Copy: A patient sees her OB monthly through pregnancy. Between those visits, SuppVis holds the whole story: the prenatal she takes daily and the medications alongside it, every combination screened as it's logged. Her walks, her yoga, the week she stopped exercising. The mornings nausea hit, the dizzy spells, the appetite swings, each logged as an incident the day it happened with everything that preceded it, not recalled weeks later. Compliance notes unchanged from v1: no outcome language, interactions "screened and flagged for discussion with her care team," one of two vignettes, Connor reviews word by word.

## Section 6: Vignette two, the incident nobody could place

Header: **Irregular symptoms have regular patterns more often than they seem.**

Copy: A patient gets dizzy spells. No schedule, no obvious trigger, no answer. Alone, each episode is an anecdote. Logged as incidents, they become a record. For every episode, the brief shows what surrounded it: what was taken that day and the days before, including the herb whose secondary mechanism nobody thought to mention. Sleep and recovery from their wearable. Habits kept and skipped. What they said was different that day. SuppVis proposes the timing associations, labeled as associations, never conclusions. Instead of starting from "it happens randomly," you start from the pattern. The judgment call stays where it belongs: with you.

## Section 7: The data layers (full platform, no roadmap band)

Header: **Everything that moves a patient, in one record.**

Grid of eight tiles:
1. **Supplements and stacks.** Full products, real doses, brands and blends resolved. Plan versus actual, day by day.
2. **Medications.** Logged and screened against supplements for interactions. SuppVis never advises on medications; it gives you the complete list and the flags. [Exact framing non-negotiable]
3. **Habits and lifestyle.** Research-backed habits, goal-mapped, with wearable-detected activity filling the record automatically.
4. **Incidents and daily scores.** Symptoms and out-of-the-ordinary events with severity and context, plus daily scores across the patient's goals: the subjective baseline everything else is read against.
5. **Wearable biometrics.** Sleep, HRV, and activity from the devices patients already wear, alongside the logged record.
6. **Bloodwork over time.** Lab results trended across draws, shown exactly as the patient's lab reported them, never recomputed, with the logged record alongside. When a patient switches labs, the portal notes why values may not be comparable.
7. **Food log.** What they ate and when, so diet changes show up next to what followed them.
8. **Cycle tracking.** Period, flow, and cycle day on the timeline for patients who choose it, read from Apple Health where they already track it, so symptoms and scores line up with the cycle.

## Section 8: Between visits (new section, from Connor's vision)

Header: **A window, not another inbox.**

Copy: Between visits, the portal is a window into how the patient is actually doing, not a control panel and not a monitoring obligation. Open any patient's timeline when you choose, flag anything for the next visit, and your flags surface at the top of their next brief. Alerts are your choice: all activity, safety only, or none. Safety alerts fire on one thing only: the patient adds something that interacts or is contraindicated with what they already take. Stepping in between visits is reserved for danger.

[Why this section exists: it answers the two objections every clinician has about remote data, "I don't have time to watch dashboards" and "does this create a duty to monitor." The copy must keep the alert scope narrow and the clinician in control. Run this section past counsel alongside the sharing model, since alert design touches standard-of-care expectations.]

## Section 9: Evidence and honesty

Header: **Built on evidence. Honest about uncertainty.**

Copy: Every line in the brief links back to the log or record it came from. The AI writes the sentences, never the facts. Every insight traces to a research library of 33,000+ peer-reviewed studies, browsable publicly at suppvis.health/research. Associations are labeled as associations, never causes. And when the record isn't sufficient to say something, SuppVis says so, including on its own safety rules: any flag from a rule still awaiting clinical review is labeled unreviewed. You've seen tools that always have an answer. This one tells the truth instead.

## Section 13: Footer boundary (verbatim, non-negotiable)

SuppVis provides wellness information and decision support for licensed professionals and their patients. It does not diagnose, treat, cure, or prevent any disease, and it never replaces clinical judgment. Associations shown in SuppVis reflect timing in the patient's own record, not causation. Medication information is used for safety screening and analysis context only; SuppVis never advises on medications.

## Language rules (updated)

- Allowed verbs: supports, is associated with, correlates with, surfaces, proposes, may help, evidence suggests
- Banned anywhere: diagnose and diagnosis-adjacent claims, detect the cause, treats, prevents, cures, will improve, guarantees, monitors (use "shows" or "a window into"; "monitoring" invites standard-of-care duty readings)
- The clinician is always the actor of judgment; SuppVis is always the instrument; the brief "stops where clinical judgment starts"
- Never on the page: January dates or any dates, NUHS or any school or campus clinic by name, sample-patient details, internal jargon ("blissors," "determinants of health form"), counsel-review status, HIPAA claims, pricing, patient counts, named practices
- Approved numbers only: 33,000+ studies, 89 research-backed habits (update if the catalog count changed), 320+ conditions, 453 supplements (update when corpus expansion ships)
